## Winners vs low performers
- The three biggest outliers **show** something (a live playground, a drawn map, the creator's own app being poked); the two low
  performers **read** (a blog post, other people's tweets). Honest fine print alone (Rithesh) didn't travel without a demo.
- Reach leaders open on the paradox or a counter-trend; the paradox is now used by 5 of 10, so it's saturated as a whole hook.
  We open on a **result** (our SMS, decided) and use the paradox as the second line.
- Greeting-first openings (Krish, Syntax) only work with a big existing audience. Our house format keeps "Hi everyone, I'm XXXX",
  so it goes **after** the hook, never before it (as in both reference scripts).
- Outliers run 7–16 min; 30–90 min only works for personal brands. Our sessions published at 11–33 min.

## What our own sessions teach (script vs video, 2026-09-30)
- Presenters keep the sayable parts word for word (hooks, analogies, stories, outros) and cut dense spec lists first.
- The hands-on was never scripted and became half the runtime, with dead air and loose ends → this script writes the
  hands-on narration out (W1) and moves the first demo before ~25% of the runtime (W2).
- Broken back-references and unpaid agenda promises came from reordering → a promise ledger in `production-notes.md` (W6, W7).

## Gaps nobody covers (our edge)
1. Independent evidence: the 7,977-item cost study and the 32.5% renamed-options flip.
2. A measured cascade (CMU: 99.6% of GPT-6's accuracy at 47% of its fee).
3. A threshold trade-off shown on one example.
4. Indian English and Hinglish messages.
5. A failure demonstrated on screen, not just described.

## Winning archetype, length and pace
_Launch with one Concept chapter · session 16–20 min recorded · ~150 wpm on camera · a visual change every 5–10 s._

## Our angle (one sentence)
Jev is a near-instant, almost-free decision model that's less accurate than an LLM, so you test it, trust it only when it's
sure, and hand the rest to an LLM.
