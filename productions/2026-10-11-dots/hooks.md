**Pick:** #1 (19/20), because it leads with the result (the Launch rule: "the result first"), works on mute (a 3:00 AM clock and a
bug card landing in a chat), and it is OpenAI's own example of a dot's job ("A bug appears in Slack, and dots immediately start
investigating", S15; "Scope a change, write code, and test it so you have working changes to review", S14). Accuracy is 4, not 5,
because it is an illustration, not a recorded run: the script says so out loud in beat 6 ("In OpenAI's own example") and the
frame carries an "illustrative" chip. #6 is stronger on curiosity but leads with the catch before the viewer knows what a dot is;
its image survives as the catch scene (the toggle switched off, the notes that stay). #4's "its own computer" is beat 5.

**First frame (described):** the house navy block. The dot's chat window, a white rounded vector replica (x 40–1040, y 240–1120:
a grey left rail with five line icons, a chat column holding one blue user bubble "Goal: keep the checkout bug-free", a plain blue
circle avatar with the chip "Dot · idle", a composer reading "Send a message"), is already open and lit. Top-left, over the window's
head, a giant mono clock "3:00 AM" in yellow (Anton, 170 px) with its colon blinking from the first frame. Nothing is empty: at 0.3 s a
grey message card "Slack · #bugs — Checkout fails on iOS" slides in from the right edge into the chat, and a small "illustrative" chip
sits under the window. Readable on mute: a chat at 3 AM, a bug arriving.

**How the video pays it off:** beats 5–8 show the dot doing the job on its own cloud computer pane under the chat (writes the fix,
tests pass, "sped up" declared) and the same "The fix is ready for review" bubble with its "Review the fix" link lands again on the
